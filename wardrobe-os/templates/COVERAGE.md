# What we know about the closet, and what we don't

The catalog says **what is there**. This file says **how complete the catalog is**.

The difference is not academic. "There is no belt in the catalog" and "you own no belt"
are two different facts, and a system that confuses them will send you shopping for
something that is in your drawer. That is a mistake this system has actually made —
which is why this file exists.

## Three states

- **`complete`** — everything in this category has been photographed and entered.
  A gap here is real and may be written into `SHOPPING-LIST.md`.
- **`partial`** — some of it is in the catalog, some definitely exists but isn't shot.
  A gap is **not proven**.
- **`unknown`** — never asked. We know nothing.

## ⛔ The main rule

> A category in state `partial` or `unknown` **cannot** produce a line in the shopping
> list. It produces **a question**.
> A purchase is recommended only where the state is `complete` and the gap is visible
> in the catalog.

The same holds when handing over an outfit: "there is no such pair in the catalog" — fine.
"You don't have shoes like that" — not allowed unless the state is `complete`.

And the reverse failure is just as bad: zero items in a category is **not** "they own none".
It is "we never asked".

## How a row moves to `complete`

Only one way: someone asked **"is that everything you own in this category, or is there
more?"** and got a yes.

⛔ Not by uploading photos. The difference between "I shot everything" and "I shot what
was lying out" is the difference between "buy a belt" and "check whether you own a belt".

Jewelry and shoes are where this goes wrong most often — the everyday pieces are being
worn, so they are never in the photos, so the catalog concludes they do not exist.
